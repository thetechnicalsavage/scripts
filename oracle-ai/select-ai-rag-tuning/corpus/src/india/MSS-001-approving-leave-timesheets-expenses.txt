## 1. What this guide covers
Your approval responsibilities as a manager in MyMeridian, what you are actually certifying when you approve, and how to delegate when you are away.

## 2. Your worklist
**MyMeridian > Manager > My Worklist** is the single place where every item awaiting you appears: leave requests, timesheets, expense claims, salary advance requests, outside engagement requests, resignation acknowledgements and confirmation reviews.

| Column | What to look at |
| Age | How long the item has waited. Items past SLA are highlighted |
| Type | The process |
| Employee | Who raised it |
| Impact | The days, the amount, or the effective date |
| Flags | System warnings: notice period breach, entitlement exceeded, duplicate, policy exception |

## 3. Approval service levels
| Item | Your SLA | What happens if you miss it |
| Leave request | 3 working days | Escalates to your manager at 5 working days |
| Timesheet | 3 working days from submission | Locks at 14 days; the employee needs an AskHR ticket to reopen |
| Expense claim | 3 working days | Escalates to your manager at 7 working days |
| Salary advance | 2 working days | Escalates to the HR Business Partner |
| Confirmation review | Due 15 days before probation ends | The employee is confirmed automatically if no decision is recorded within 15 days of the probation end date |

## 4. What you are certifying
Approval is not an acknowledgement. When you approve you are confirming, on the record:

- **Leave**: the absence is workable, the cover is arranged, and you have no reason to doubt the stated purpose.
- **Timesheet**: the hours and the project allocation reflect work actually performed. Timesheets drive client invoicing, so an inaccurate approval can mean an incorrect invoice to a client.
- **Expense claim**: the expense was incurred for Meridian business, it is within the entitlement or you have approved the exception, and the business purpose is genuine.
- **Salary advance**: you are aware of the request. You are not assessing the employee's personal circumstances and you should not ask about them. Eligibility is checked by the system and by HR.

Approving without looking is the single most common control failure found in the internal audit of expenses.

## 5. Handling leave requests
- Check the team calendar at **MyMeridian > Manager > Team Calendar** before deciding, to see overlapping absence.
- You may decline a request, but you must give a reason, and for a restricted holiday under HRP-006 you must offer an alternative date.
- You may **not** decline Sick Leave, or emergency Casual Leave, on business grounds.
- If you want the employee to change the dates, use **Return for amendment** rather than Reject. Rejecting makes the employee start again and loses the history.
- A request that breaches the notice period in HRP-001 shows a flag. You can still approve it; the flag records that you were aware.

## 6. Handling expense claims
- Check the business purpose, not only the amount. A correct amount with no stated purpose is not approvable.
- Check the entitlement panel shown against each line. Where a line exceeds the entitlement, look for the prior approval; if there is none, return the claim.
- Use **Partial approval** to approve valid lines and query one line, rather than holding the whole claim.
- Do not approve your own claim through a delegation you hold. The system blocks it, but do not attempt a workaround.
- Claims over INR 50,000 additionally route to your function head after your approval.

## 7. Timesheets
- Approve weekly, by the end of Tuesday for the previous week.
- Look for: hours booked to a project the employee is not staffed on, a full week booked while the employee was on approved leave, and overtime hours that were not pre-approved under HRP-008.
- Where a correction is needed, return the timesheet with a comment. Do not edit it yourself; the employee must resubmit so that the record shows who asserted the hours.

## 8. Delegating while you are away
Set this up **before** you go: **MyMeridian > My Profile > Delegation**.

- Choose the delegate, normally a peer manager or your own manager.
- Set the start and end dates. Delegation ends automatically; there is no need to remember to turn it off.
- Choose which processes to delegate. You can delegate leave and timesheets while retaining expense and advance approvals.
- Your delegate sees the items in their own worklist, marked as delegated from you.
- **Accountability does not transfer.** Approvals are recorded in both names.

If you go away without setting a delegate, items escalate to your manager after the SLA, which is a poorer outcome for your team.

## 9. Bulk actions
The worklist supports selecting multiple items of the same type and approving them together. Bulk approval is appropriate for routine timesheets from a stable team. It is **not** appropriate for expense claims, salary advances or any item carrying a flag; the system requires those to be opened individually.
