#BODY
## 1. Purpose and legal basis
This policy explains how Meridian Systems India Private Limited handles personal data. It gives effect to the **Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023** and, where Meridian processes data of individuals in other jurisdictions on behalf of clients, to the applicable law of that jurisdiction, including the EU General Data Protection Regulation.

Under the DPDP Act, Meridian is a **Data Fiduciary** in respect of its own employees' data, and usually a **Data Processor** in respect of client data. An individual whose data is processed is a **Data Principal**.

## 2. Employee personal data that Meridian holds
| Category | Examples | Purpose |
| Identity and contact | Name, date of birth, photograph, address, personal email and phone | Employment administration, statutory reporting |
| Statutory identifiers | PAN, Aadhaar, UAN, ESIC number, passport | Payroll, provident fund, tax, immigration |
| Employment record | Grade, role, reporting line, location, appraisal ratings, disciplinary record | Managing the employment relationship |
| Financial | Bank account, salary, tax declarations, reimbursement claims | Payment of salary and reimbursements |
| Family and dependants | Spouse and children's names and dates of birth, nominee details | Insurance, provident fund and gratuity nominations |
| Health | Medical certificates, insurance claims, accommodation assessments | Leave administration, insurance, reasonable accommodation |
| Background verification | Education, previous employment, criminal record check, address check | Pre-employment verification under HRP-035 |
| System and access | Access card logs, network and application logs, device telemetry | Security, safety and asset protection |

Health data and background verification data are held separately from the general personnel file, with access restricted to named roles.

## 3. Principles applied
- **Lawful purpose and notice.** Data is collected for a stated, lawful purpose and the employee is given a notice describing it.
- **Purpose limitation.** Data collected for one purpose is not used for an unrelated one. Attendance data, for example, is not used to evaluate performance.
- **Data minimisation.** Only what is necessary is collected. Meridian does not ask for marital status at recruitment, does not ask for a photograph before the offer stage, and does not collect caste or religion except where a statutory return requires it.
- **Accuracy.** Employees can correct their own data through ESS-003.
- **Storage limitation.** Data is deleted at the end of the retention period in section 6.
- **Security.** Technical and organisational safeguards are applied under HRP-033.
- **Accountability.** The Data Protection Officer maintains a record of processing activities.

## 4. Rights of employees as Data Principals
| Right | How to exercise it | Response time |
| Access a summary of personal data processed | AskHR ticket, Privacy category | 30 days |
| Correction, completion or updating | Self-service in MyMeridian, or AskHR ticket | 15 days |
| Erasure, where the purpose is served and no legal obligation requires retention | AskHR ticket, Privacy category | 30 days |
| Nominate a person to exercise rights in the event of death or incapacity | MyMeridian > My Profile > Nominations | Immediate |
| Grievance about the handling of personal data | Data Protection Officer, then the Data Protection Board of India | 30 days |
| Withdraw consent, where processing is based on consent | AskHR ticket | 15 days |

Where a request is refused, the reason is given in writing. The most common reason is a statutory retention obligation: payroll and provident fund records, for example, cannot be erased on request.

## 5. Consent and its limits
Much of Meridian's processing of employee data does not rely on consent, because the DPDP Act permits processing for **certain legitimate uses**, including employment purposes such as preventing corporate espionage, protecting intellectual property, maintaining confidentiality of trade secrets, and providing a service or benefit to the employee. Where processing does rely on consent, for example the inclusion of a photograph in a public marketing page, the consent is specific, recorded, and can be withdrawn without any detriment.

## 6. Retention
| Record | Retention period |
| Personnel file, contract, appraisal history | 8 years after separation |
| Payroll and tax records | 8 years after the end of the financial year |
| Provident fund records | As required by the EPFO, currently permanent for the member record |
| Recruitment records for unsuccessful candidates | 12 months from the decision, unless the candidate consents to a longer talent-pool retention |
| Background verification reports | 3 years after separation |
| Disciplinary and investigation records | 8 years after closure |
| Internal Committee records under HRP-024 | As prescribed by the rules |
| System access and security logs | 12 months |
| CCTV footage | 30 days, unless retained for an investigation |

At the end of the period, records are securely deleted. Deletion is logged.

## 7. Sharing personal data
Employee data is shared only with: statutory authorities where required; the payroll processor, insurer, third-party administrator, background verification agency and benefits providers under contract; and a client where the contract requires named-personnel disclosure, limited to name, role, grade and the verification status.

Every processor is bound by a written contract containing confidentiality, security and breach-notification obligations, and is assessed before engagement and annually thereafter.

## 8. Cross-border transfer
Personal data may be transferred outside India except to a country restricted by notification of the Central Government. Where a client's contract imposes stricter data residency terms, those terms apply and are recorded in the engagement's data handling note. Employees must never move client personal data to a location or a service not listed in that note.

## 9. Personal data breach
A personal data breach is any unauthorised processing, or accidental disclosure, acquisition, sharing, use, alteration, destruction or loss of access to personal data.

- Report a suspected breach to the IT service desk and the Data Protection Officer **immediately, and no later than 1 hour** after becoming aware. Do not attempt to investigate or contain it alone.
- Meridian notifies the **Data Protection Board of India and each affected Data Principal without delay**, in the form prescribed.
- Where the breach concerns client data, the client is notified within the timeline in the contract, which is commonly 24 hours.
- An employee who reports a breach promptly, including one they caused themselves, will not face disciplinary action for the error itself. **Concealing a breach is serious misconduct.**

## 10. Data Protection Officer
The Data Protection Officer can be contacted through an AskHR ticket in the Privacy category or at the address published in MyMeridian > My Compliance. The Data Protection Officer reports to the Board on privacy matters.
